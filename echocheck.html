The problem

In an echoey or noisy classroom, students miss words. It hits younger students, second-language learners and anyone with hearing loss hardest. Acoustic consultants measure this with expensive kit. EchoCheck does a screening audit with a phone and says what fix is needed.

How it's measured

  1. Background noise. 30 s of microphone signal in 4,096-sample blocks. Each block is Fourier-transformed and A-weighted (IEC 61672). Leq is the energy average, L90 the level exceeded 90 % of the time, which is the steady floor from fans and traffic.
  2. Echo time (RT60). A clap is an impulse. The capture is filtered into octave bands (250 Hz–4 kHz). Schroeder backward integration (ISO 3382-2) gives the decay curve, with the noise subtracted. A line fitted from −5 to −25 dB (T20) is extrapolated to 60 dB. The headline value is the mean of 500 Hz and 1 kHz.
  3. Clarity C50. 10·log₁₀(energy in the first 50 ms / energy after), at 1 kHz.
  4. STI. For 7 octave bands × 14 modulation frequencies: m(F) = 1/√(1+(2πF·T/13.8)²) × 1/(1+10^(−SNR/10)). That turns into an apparent SNR, clipped to ±15 dB, then a transmission index, and the bands are weighted and summed. SNR = 60 dB(A) speech − background Leq. Bands outside 250 Hz–4 kHz reuse the nearest measured echo time.
  5. Fix. Sabine: A = 0.161·V / T. Extra absorption = 0.161·V·(1/Tlimit − 1/Tnow). Panel area = that ÷ 0.75 (a 0.8-absorbing panel replacing a surface absorbing about 0.05).
  6. Limits. ANSI S12.60-2010: echo time ≤ 0.6 s up to 283 m³ and ≤ 0.7 s up to 566 m³; background noise ≤ 35 dB(A).

Limits, honestly

  • A screening audit, not a certified measurement. Uncalibrated noise levels are ±5 dB, and the STI uses an assumed voice level.
  • A clap gives 40–50 dB of decay range, enough for T20 but not T30. Bass bands are less certain; a balloon pop works better.
  • Measure at 2–3 spots with the room set up as normally used.
